## 2026-08-22 — The robot lions become a Teambuilder

### Declaration

The working session began with a coordination problem expressed through five mighty robot lions.

Five robot lions require five human pilots. The lions begin parked. A participant chooses a lion, receives a lion key by email, and presses the individual lion into battle mode. A 72-hour robeast clock makes coordination consequential: attacks can buy time, better synchronization produces stronger results, and only the highest level of coordination permits the pilots to form Voltron and defeat the robeast permanently.

This established the broader design direction. The lions are not merely illustrations or buttons. They are participants in a coordination engine measuring presence, concurrence, and synchronization.

### Construction

A working GitHub repository, `ourlovelysystem/lovely-system-teambuilder`, was created and developed in a rapid series of commits.

Yellow Lion became the first implemented experience primitive. The participant enters a parked lion with no manual and proceeds through a controlled sequence:

`PARKED → COCKPIT → SIT → STAND → WIND-UP → ROAR → SHOCKWAVE → CRITICAL SYSTEMS FAILURE → MEOW → SPLAT → CONTINUE`

The controls were refined to include the cockpit choice `RETREAT` or `ATTACK BACKWARD`, the release command `F-U-L-L P-O-W-E-R`, and an arcade-style demand to `INSERT 25 CENTS TO CONTINUE`. Approved onboarding assets were organized into the YL-001 and YL-002 batches; approved token assets followed as YL-003. The continuation screen recognizes pennies, nickels, dimes, quarters, half dollars, and a manhole cover while remaining very particular about the requested twenty-five cents.

The landing page was rebuilt around the approved Ernie background and compacted around the declaration:

> When someone asks you to pilot a mighty robot lion juggernaut, you say Y-E-S-!

The experience retained its operating contract:

> Participant controls action. System controls sequence.

### Catastrophic failure

The failure frame was treated as consequence rather than decoration. After the roar and its directional shockwave, the cockpit becomes red, loud, and overwhelming. Critical systems fail, hydraulic fluid is empty, mobility is lost, and the lion's mighty exterior performance ends in an uncertain meow and collapse.

The final catastrophic-failure binary replaced:

`assets/experiences/yellow-lion-onboarding/approved/11-critical-systems-failure.png`

The change was committed locally, rebased over intervening remote work, pushed successfully, and confirmed synchronized with `origin/main`.

### Deployment

The application was deployed at `teambuilder.ourlovelysystem.org`, publishing the landing page and Yellow Lion Onboarding at `/yellow/`.

The working session therefore moved the project from an idea about coordinated robot-lion pilots to a public, executable experience.

### Conceptual expansion

After the lion acquired a domain, the session widened beyond the Teambuilder implementation.

A concept was declared: **scarce web objects**. A lion could exist on one site and be taken by a person to another. Its image could be reproduced, but its recognized presence could remain scarce. The site would be a place the lion visits, not the lion's owner.

> In the jungle, the-jungle.com, the lion sleeps tonight.  
> Tomorrow, maybe not.

From this followed **stray web animals**: persistent scarce objects without a current holder or recognized home. Then came **web ghosts**: persistent identities whose holder, home, purpose, or original living object no longer exists.

These portability, scarcity, stray-animal, and ghost concepts were declared during the session. They were not implemented in this deployment.

### Result

One lion became operational. Its failure became visible. Its consequences became funny. Its repository became durable. Its domain became public.

The lions also escaped the conceptual boundary of a single website.

**Final status:** Yellow Lion is deployed. Voltron remains a coordination condition. Scarce web animals have been declared.
